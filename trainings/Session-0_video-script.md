## Keywords and Concepts (2 minutes)

[SCREEN: Bulleted list of keywords appears one by one as narrator speaks]

**Narrator:**

"Before we dive in, let's cover some key terms you'll need to know.

**First: Cloud Storage.** This means your files are stored on Google's servers, not just on your computer. You can access them from any device with internet—your office computer, your laptop, even your phone. And if your computer crashes, your files are safe in the cloud.

**Second: Sharing Permissions.** When you share a file or folder, you control who can see it and what they can do. You can give someone view-only access, or allow them to edit. This replaces the need to email files back and forth.

**Third: Labels.** In Gmail, labels work like folders—but better. An email can have multiple labels, making it easier to organize and find. Think of them as color-coded sticky notes for your emails.

**Fourth: Real-Time Collaboration.** Multiple people can work on the same document at the same time. You can see each other's changes instantly. No more emailing different versions back and forth.

**Fifth: 2-Step Verification.** This is an extra layer of security for your account. When you sign in, you'll need both your password AND a code sent to your phone. This keeps your account secure."

[SCREEN: Visual examples of each concept appear as they're mentioned]

[SCREEN: Back to Gmail interface]

**Narrator:**

"Now let's look at Gmail features.

To compose an email, click the Compose button. Enter the recipient's email in the To field. Use CC to include others who need to see the email, and BCC to include recipients without others seeing their addresses.

Add a clear subject line, type your message, and attach files using the paperclip icon. Then click Send.

To organize your emails, use labels. On the left sidebar, click the plus sign next to Labels to create a new one. Name it something descriptive, like 'Urgent' or 'Project A.' To label an email, select it, click the Label icon, and choose the label.

To find emails, use the search bar at the top. Type keywords like the sender's name, subject, or date. Gmail will show all matching emails."

### Google Drive (2.5 minutes)

[SCREEN: Screen recording of Google Drive interface]

**Narrator:**

"Google Drive is your digital filing cabinet. To access it, go to drive.google.com.

To create a folder, click New, then Folder. Give it a descriptive name. I recommend using a numbering system to keep folders in order, like '01_Official_Documents' or '02_Projects.'

Inside your folder, you can create subfolders to organize files further.

To upload files, click New, then File upload, or simply drag and drop files from your computer into Google Drive.

To share a file or folder, right-click it and select Share. Enter the email addresses of people you want to share with, and choose their access level: Viewer, Commenter, or Editor.

You can also generate a shareable link. Click Share, then 'Change to anyone with the link,' choose the access level, and copy the link to share.

To mark important items, right-click and select Add star. Starred items appear in the Starred section on the left, making them easy to find.

You can also color-code folders by right-clicking and selecting Change color."

### Google Calendar (2 minutes)

[SCREEN: Screen recording of Google Calendar interface]

**Narrator:**

"Google Calendar is your digital wall calendar. Go to calendar.google.com to access it.

To create an event, click on any date and time slot. Enter the event title, date, and time. Add a description with details about the event.

To invite others, click Add guests and enter their email addresses. They'll receive an invitation and can respond.

Set reminders so you never miss an event. You can choose to be reminded minutes, hours, or days before.

You can view your calendar in different ways: Day view shows one day, Week view shows the entire week, and Month view shows the entire month.

To share your calendar, hover over your calendar name on the left, click the three dots, then Settings and sharing. Under Share with specific people, click Add people, enter email addresses, and choose permission levels."

### Google Chat vs Gmail (1 minute)

[SCREEN: Split screen showing Gmail on left, Google Chat on right]

**Narrator:**

"Let's clarify when to use Google Chat versus Gmail.

Google Chat is for quick, real-time conversations with colleagues. It's like texting or instant messaging. Use it when you need an immediate response.

Gmail is for formal, documented communication. Use it when you need a record of the conversation, when sending attachments, or when the recipient may not be available immediately.

Think of it this way: Google Chat is like walking over to a colleague's desk for a quick question. Gmail is like sending a formal memo or letter."

### LibreOffice Overview (1.5 minutes)

[SCREEN: LibreOffice logo and icons for Writer, Calc, Impress]

**Narrator:**

"Finally, let's talk about LibreOffice. LibreOffice is a free, open-source office suite that will be deployed campus-wide. It serves as an offline backup when you don't have internet access.

LibreOffice Writer is similar to Microsoft Word or Google Docs. You use it for creating documents.

LibreOffice Calc is similar to Microsoft Excel or Google Sheets. You use it for spreadsheets and data.

LibreOffice Impress is similar to Microsoft PowerPoint or Google Slides. You use it for presentations.

How does it work with Google Workspace? You can download Google files as LibreOffice formats—.odt for documents, .ods for spreadsheets, .odp for presentations. Open them in LibreOffice to work offline. When internet returns, upload them back to Google Drive, and Google will convert them back.

LibreOffice will be installed on all PSHS computers before the full training rollout. For now, just know it exists as your offline backup."

## Production Notes for Video Recording

### Technical Requirements
- **Resolution:** 1920x1080 (1080p) minimum
- **Audio:** Clear narration, no background noise
- **Screen Recording:** Use zoom-in features for small text and buttons
- **Pacing:** Speak clearly and at a moderate pace; allow pauses for viewers to process information

### Visual Style
- **Color Scheme:** Use PSHS branding colors where appropriate
- **Font:** Clean, readable sans-serif (Arial, Roboto, or similar)
- **Transitions:** Simple cuts or fades; avoid flashy transitions
- **Annotations:** Use arrows, circles, or highlights to draw attention to specific elements

### Accessibility
- **Captions:** Include closed captions for accessibility
- **Visual Cues:** Use visual indicators to reinforce concepts
- **Pacing:** Allow time for viewers to read on-screen text before moving on

### Distribution
- **Format:** MP4 or WebM
- **File Size:** Compress to under 500MB for easy sharing via email or Google Drive
- **Hosting:** Upload to Google Drive and share link 2-3 days before the in-person session
- **Deadline:** Ensure video is available to participants at least 48 hours before the session

---

## Important Notes for the Trainer

1. **Emphasize PSHS Email Policies:** Spend extra time on FAM 12.2 Section 3.2, especially 3.2.4. This is critical for compliance.

2. **Use PSHS Examples:** When demonstrating features, use PSHS-specific examples (e.g., creating a folder for "2026_Official_Documents," labeling emails as "Urgent" or "Training").

3. **Mention LibreOffice Deployment:** Clarify that LibreOffice will be installed campus-wide before the full training rollout. It's optional but useful as a backup.

4. **Highlight Real-Time Collaboration:** Emphasize that multiple people can work on the same document simultaneously—this is a key benefit of Google Workspace.

5. **Remind About Security:** Briefly mention 2-step verification and keeping passwords secure, as per FAM 12.2 Section 3.1.7.
