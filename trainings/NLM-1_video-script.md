## Keywords and Concepts (2 minutes)

[SCREEN: Bulleted list of keywords appears one by one as narrator speaks]

**Narrator:**

"Before we dive in, let's cover some key terms you'll need to know.

**First: NotebookLM.** This is Google's free AI-powered research assistant. It can analyze documents, websites, and audio files to help you understand and summarize information. For our purposes, it's the tool that will transform your meeting recordings into structured meeting minutes.

**Second: Audio Source.** In NotebookLM, an audio source is simply the audio file you upload—your meeting recording. NotebookLM processes this audio and makes it searchable and queryable.

**Third: AI Summary.** When you upload audio to NotebookLM, it automatically generates a summary of what was discussed. This is your starting point for meeting minutes—a quick overview of the meeting's content.

**Fourth: Prompt.** A prompt is a question or instruction you give to NotebookLM. By asking the right questions, you can extract specific information like action items, decisions, or discussion points.

**Fifth: Action Items.** These are tasks that need to be completed after the meeting—things that were assigned to specific people with deadlines. Capturing these accurately is crucial for meeting follow-up.

**Sixth: Decisions.** These are the outcomes of discussions—the agreements, approvals, or conclusions reached during the meeting. These are often the most important part of meeting minutes."

## Pattern Demonstration (8-10 minutes)

### Introduction to NotebookLM (1.5 minutes)

[SCREEN: NotebookLM start screen and interface]

**Narrator:**

"Let's start with an overview of NotebookLM. When you open notebooklm.google.com and sign in with your Google account, you'll see the main interface.

NotebookLM is organized around 'notebooks'—each notebook is like a folder for a specific project or topic. For meeting minutes, you'll typically create one notebook per meeting.

In each notebook, you can add multiple 'sources'—these are the documents, websites, or audio files that NotebookLM will analyze. For meeting minutes, your primary source will be the meeting audio recording.

Once you add a source, NotebookLM automatically processes it and generates several outputs:
- A Summary of the content
- Key Topics that were covered
- And you can ask it questions using prompts

Let's see this in action with a real example."

### Recording Meeting Audio on Your Phone (2 minutes)

[SCREEN: Screen recording of a smartphone with a voice recorder app]

**Narrator:**

"Before we can use NotebookLM, we need to record our meeting. Most smartphones have a built-in voice recorder app. Let me show you how to use it.

First, open your voice recorder app. On most phones, you'll find it in your apps list.

Tap the record button—usually a red circle. The app will start recording and show a timer.

For the best results:
- Place your phone near the center of the table
- Choose a quiet room if possible
- Ask participants to speak clearly and one at a time
- Start recording before the meeting begins to capture introductions

When the meeting is over, tap the stop button. The app will save your recording.

Give the file a clear name—something like 'Staff Meeting April 17 2025'—so you can easily find it later.

Finally, transfer the audio file to your computer. You can email it to yourself, upload to Google Drive, or use a USB cable.

Now you have your meeting audio ready to upload to NotebookLM."

### Uploading Audio to NotebookLM (2 minutes)

[SCREEN: Screen recording of NotebookLM interface]

**Narrator:**

"Now let's upload our audio to NotebookLM.

First, go to notebooklm.google.com and sign in with your Google account.

Click 'Create' to start a new notebook. Give it a clear name—I'll call this one 'Staff Meeting April 17 2025'.

Now click 'Add Source'. You'll see options for different types of sources. Select 'Audio'.

Click 'Upload' and choose your audio file from your computer.

Wait for the upload to complete. You'll see a progress indicator. The time it takes depends on the file size—typically a few minutes for a standard meeting.

Once the upload is complete, NotebookLM will process the audio. You'll see it analyzing the file.

When processing is finished, you'll see the audio source listed in your notebook, and NotebookLM will have automatically generated a Summary and Key Topics.

That's it! Your meeting audio is now in NotebookLM and ready to analyze."

### Generating Summaries and Extracting Notes (2.5 minutes)

[SCREEN: Screen recording showing Summary, Key Topics, and Audio Overview features]

**Narrator:**

"Now let's explore what NotebookLM has generated from our meeting audio.

First, look at the **Summary**. This is an automatically generated overview of what was discussed in the meeting. It includes the main topics, key points, and conclusions. This is your starting point for meeting minutes.

Next, click on **Key Topics**. NotebookLM has identified the main themes discussed in the meeting. Each topic is a clickable link—click on one, and NotebookLM will show you relevant excerpts from the audio where that topic was discussed.

One of NotebookLM's unique features is the **Audio Overview**. Click 'Generate Audio Overview', and NotebookLM will create a podcast-style summary of your meeting—about 2-3 minutes long. You can listen to this to get a quick sense of what was discussed.

You can also view the original audio source. Click on the audio file in your notebook, and you'll see the full recording with timestamps. You can jump to any point in the meeting.

But the real power of NotebookLM comes from asking it questions. Let me show you how to use prompts to extract specific information."

### Prompting for Meeting Minutes (2 minutes)

[SCREEN: Screen recording showing prompt input and responses with citations]

**Narrator:**

"Prompting is how you get NotebookLM to give you exactly what you need for meeting minutes. Let's try some useful prompts.

First, let's extract action items. I'll type: 'List all action items from this meeting, including what needs to be done, who is responsible, and any deadlines mentioned.'

[Type the prompt and show the response]

NotebookLM has listed all the action items from the meeting, with responsible persons and deadlines. Notice the small numbers in brackets—these are citations. If I click on one, it takes me to the exact point in the audio where this action item was discussed. This is incredibly useful for verification.

Now let's extract decisions. I'll type: 'List all decisions made during this meeting, including what was decided and the context.'

[Type the prompt and show the response]

Again, NotebookLM provides a structured list of decisions with citations.

You can also ask for summaries by topic. For example: 'Summarize the discussion about the budget.'

[Type the prompt and show the response]

NotebookLM gives me a focused summary of just that topic, with relevant citations.

The key is to be specific with your prompts. The clearer your question, the better the response."

### Refining Output into Google Docs (1.5 minutes)

[SCREEN: Screen recording showing Google Docs with meeting minutes]

**Narrator:**

"Now let's turn all this information into a formal meeting minutes document.

I'll open a new Google Doc and give it a title: 'Staff Meeting Minutes - April 17, 2025'.

First, I'll add a header with the meeting details: date, time, location, chairperson, and attendees. I can get the attendees from NotebookLM by asking: 'List all participants who spoke during the meeting.'

[Show copying information from NotebookLM to Google Docs]

Now I'll copy the Summary from NotebookLM and paste it into my document as the introduction.

Next, I'll copy the Key Topics and use them to structure the main body of the minutes.

I'll add a section for Action Items, copying the list from NotebookLM and formatting it as a table with columns for the item, responsible person, and deadline.

Finally, I'll add a section for Decisions, copying from NotebookLM.

With just a few minutes of copying and formatting, I've gone from a raw audio recording to a polished meeting minutes document.

Compare this to the traditional process of listening to the entire recording, taking notes, transcribing, and formatting—which could take hours.

With NotebookLM, you can issue meeting minutes within hours of the meeting ending, rather than days."

## Production Notes

### Visual Elements

- **Opening:** Use a split-screen comparison with vintage stenographer imagery vs modern AI
- **Screen Recordings:** All screen recordings should be clear, with zoom-ins on important UI elements
- **Citations:** Highlight citation numbers when they appear to draw attention to this feature
- **Transitions:** Use smooth transitions between sections
- **Text Overlays:** Use text overlays for key terms and prompts to reinforce learning

### Audio Quality

- Use a high-quality microphone for narration
- Speak clearly and at a moderate pace
- Maintain consistent volume throughout
- Consider adding background music at low volume during transitions (optional)

### Timing

- Keep each section within the specified time limits
- Allow brief pauses after key points for emphasis
- The total video should be 10-15 minutes

### Accessibility

- Add closed captions for all narration
- Use high-contrast colors for text overlays
- Ensure all on-screen text is large enough to read easily

### Technical Considerations

- Use 1080p resolution or higher
- Record screen recordings at 30fps or higher
- Test all interactive elements (clicks, scrolling) before final recording
- Have backup recordings of screen demos in case of technical issues

### Post-Production

- Edit out mistakes, long pauses, and unnecessary content
- Add visual markers (arrows, highlights) to draw attention to important UI elements
- Ensure smooth transitions between different applications (NotebookLM, Google Docs)
- Add a progress indicator or section titles to help viewers navigate

### Distribution

- Export in a common format (MP4)
- Keep file size reasonable for easy downloading/streaming
- Host on Google Drive or the PSHS learning management system
- Provide download link to participants 2-3 days before the in-person session
