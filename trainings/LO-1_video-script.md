## Keywords and Concepts (2 minutes)

[SCREEN: Bulleted list of keywords appears one by one as narrator speaks]

**Narrator:**

"Before we dive in, let's cover some key terms you'll need to know.

**First: Open Source.** LibreOffice is free and open-source software. This means it's developed by a community of volunteers and is available to everyone at no cost. No licensing fees, no subscriptions—ever.

**Second: File Formats.** Different software uses different file formats. Microsoft Word uses .docx, Google Docs uses its own format, and LibreOffice uses .odt. The good news is that LibreOffice can open and save in all of these formats.

**Third: Offline Workflow.** This is the process of working without internet. You download files when online, work on them offline in LibreOffice, then upload them back when internet returns.

**Fourth: Compatibility.** LibreOffice is compatible with both Microsoft Office and Google Workspace. You can open .docx, .xlsx, and .pptx files in LibreOffice. You can also open Google Docs, Sheets, and Slides by downloading them first.

**Fifth: Native Formats.** LibreOffice's native formats are .odt for documents, .ods for spreadsheets, and .odp for presentations. These are open standards that any software can open, ensuring your files are always accessible."

[SCREEN: Visual examples of each concept appear as they're mentioned]

## Pattern Demonstration (8-10 minutes)

### Introduction to LibreOffice (1.5 minutes)

[SCREEN: LibreOffice start screen and interface]

**Narrator:**

"Let's start with an overview of LibreOffice. When you open LibreOffice, you'll see a start screen with options to create different types of documents.

LibreOffice includes three main applications:

**Writer** is for documents—reports, letters, memos. It's like Microsoft Word or Google Docs.

**Calc** is for spreadsheets—budgets, lists, data tracking. It's like Microsoft Excel or Google Sheets.

**Impress** is for presentations—training materials, visual content. It's like Microsoft PowerPoint or Google Slides.

LibreOffice will be installed on all PSHS computers before the full training rollout. If you don't see it on your computer, contact ITU."

### LibreOffice Writer (2.5 minutes)

[SCREEN: Screen recording of LibreOffice Writer interface]

**Narrator:**

"Let's look at LibreOffice Writer first. To open it, click Start, find LibreOffice, and click Writer Document.

The interface looks very similar to Microsoft Word. You have a toolbar at the top with formatting options, a menu bar, and a large workspace for your document.

Let's create a simple document. Type a title, like 'Sample Memo.' Make it bold and centered by selecting the text and clicking the Bold and Center buttons.

Now let's add some content. Type 'To: All Staff' on one line, 'From: Your Name' on the next, and 'Date: Today's Date' on the third. Make these bold.

Now type your message below. 'This is a sample document created in LibreOffice Writer.'

To save your document, click File, then Save As. Name it 'Sample_Memo' and notice that the file extension is .odt—this is LibreOffice's native format. Click Save.

But what if you need to share this with someone using Microsoft Word? No problem. Click File, Save As again, and in the 'Save as type' dropdown, select Microsoft Word 2007-365. The file extension changes to .docx. Click Save.

Now you have two versions—one in LibreOffice format, one in Microsoft format.

[SCREEN: Downloading from Google Docs]

**Narrator:**

"To open a Google Doc in LibreOffice, open Google Drive, open any Google Doc, click File, Download, and choose OpenDocument Text. The file downloads to your computer. Double-click it, and LibreOffice Writer opens automatically. You can now edit it offline!

When internet returns, open Google Drive, click New, File upload, select your edited file, and Google converts it back to Google Docs format."

### LibreOffice Calc (2.5 minutes)

[SCREEN: Screen recording of LibreOffice Calc interface]

**Narrator:**

"Now let's look at LibreOffice Calc. To open it, click Start, find LibreOffice, and click Spreadsheet.

Again, the interface looks very similar to Microsoft Excel. You have cells arranged in rows and columns, a formula bar at the top, and toolbars for formatting.

Let's create a simple spreadsheet. In cell A1, type 'Item.' In B1, type 'Cost.' In C1, type 'Date.' Make these bold and add a background color.

Now let's add some data. In A2, type 'Office Supplies.' In B2, type '500.' In C2, type today's date.

In A3, type 'Printer Paper.' In B3, type '750.' In C3, type today's date.

Now let's calculate the total. In cell A4, type 'Total.' In cell B4, type an equals sign, then SUM, open parenthesis, B2, colon, B3, close parenthesis. Press Enter, and you'll see the total cost—1250.

To save, click File, Save As. Name it 'Budget_Tracker' and notice the file extension is .ods—LibreOffice's native spreadsheet format. Click Save.

Like Writer, you can also save as Microsoft Excel format by choosing .xlsx in the 'Save as type' dropdown.

[SCREEN: Downloading from Google Sheets]

**Narrator:**

"To open a Google Sheet in LibreOffice, open Google Drive, open any Google Sheet, click File, Download, and choose OpenDocument Spreadsheet. The file downloads. Double-click it, and LibreOffice Calc opens automatically. Edit it offline, then upload back to Google Drive when internet returns."

[SCREEN: Screen recording of LibreOffice Impress interface]

To add a new slide, click Insert, Slide, or press Control-M. For the second slide, title it 'Why LibreOffice?' and add bullet points: 'Free and open-source,' 'Works offline,' 'Compatible with Microsoft Office,' and 'Compatible with Google Workspace.'

### Offline Workflow Summary (1.5 minutes)

[SCREEN: Flowchart showing the offline workflow]

**Narrator:**

"Let's put it all together. Here's the complete offline workflow:

**When internet is available:**
1. Download important Google Docs as .odt files
2. Download important Google Sheets as .ods files
3. Download important Google Slides as .odp files
4. Save them to your computer

**When internet is down:**
1. Open LibreOffice
2. Open the file you need to work on
3. Make your edits
4. Save your changes

**When internet returns:**
1. Open Google Drive
2. Upload your edited file
3. Google converts it back to Google format
4. The file appears in your Google Drive

This workflow ensures you never lose productivity due to internet outages.

[SCREEN: Google Drive desktop app interface]

**Narrator:**

"Optional but recommended: You can install the Google Drive desktop app. This creates a Google Drive folder on your computer that syncs automatically. Go to drive.google.com, click Settings, Get Drive for desktop, download and install, sign in with your PSHS Google account, and a Google Drive folder appears on your computer. Files you save here sync automatically when online."

## Production Notes for Video Recording

### Technical Requirements
- **Resolution:** 1920x1080 (1080p) minimum
- **Audio:** Clear narration, no background noise
- **Screen Recording:** Use zoom-in features for small text and buttons
- **Pacing:** Speak clearly and at a moderate pace; allow pauses for viewers to process information

### Visual Style
- **Color Scheme:** Use LibreOffice branding colors where appropriate
- **Font:** Clean, readable sans-serif (Arial, Roboto, or similar)
- **Transitions:** Simple cuts or fades; avoid flashy transitions
- **Annotations:** Use arrows, circles, or highlights to draw attention to specific elements

### Accessibility
- **Captions:** Include closed captions for accessibility
- **Visual Cues:** Use visual indicators to reinforce concepts
- **Pacing:** Allow time for viewers to read on-screen text before moving on

### Distribution
- **Format:** MP4 or WebM
- **File Size:** Compress to under 500MB for easy sharing via email or Google Drive
- **Hosting:** Upload to Google Drive and share link 2-3 days before the in-person session
- **Deadline:** Ensure video is available to participants at least 48 hours before the session

---

## Important Notes for the Trainer

1. **Emphasize the Offline Workflow:** This is the most important concept. Participants need to understand the complete cycle: download → work offline → upload.

2. **Reassure Participants:** Many may be nervous about learning "new" software. Emphasize that LibreOffice is very similar to Microsoft Office.

3. **Focus on Native Formats:** While LibreOffice can open Microsoft formats, emphasize that .odt, .ods, and .odp are the native formats and work best.

4. **Mention Campus-Wide Deployment:** Clarify that LibreOffice will be installed on all PSHS computers before the full training rollout.

5. **Keep It Simple:** Don't get bogged down in advanced features. Focus on creating, opening, saving, and uploading—these cover 90% of use cases.

6. **Use Real Examples:** When demonstrating, use PSHS-specific examples (e.g., creating a memo, a budget tracker, a unit presentation).
